WAIT, I QUALIFY?!  —  EPISODE 3:  "Food"

SHAWNA:  Welcome back to "Wait, I Qualify?!" -- the show about unlocking aid for real people, right here in Nevada County. I'm Shawna.

JEFF:  And I'm Jeff. We're from techempower dot org -- a local nonprofit that helps folks find the help and incentives they're entitled to, from the state, the federal government, and local organizations.

SHAWNA:  Today: food. The grocery help you've already earned, the rules that just changed this year, and the places right here in town that will hand you groceries this week -- some with no paperwork at all.

JEFF:  And this one's timely. Our local food bank says recent federal changes cost about twenty-five hundred of our Nevada County neighbors their grocery benefits. So today we get people signed up, we get people back on, and we cover the safety net under the safety net. Three stops: CalFresh, W-I-C, and the no-questions-asked local food.

SHAWNA:  Let's start with the anchor. Jeff -- CalFresh, one sentence.

JEFF:  CalFresh is California's grocery money program -- what folks used to call food stamps. You get a card -- they call it an E-B-T card -- that works like a debit card at the register, it reloads every month, and you spend it on groceries at the stores where you already shop.

SHAWNA:  And who qualifies? Because I know the answer is "more people than you think" -- it's the name of the show.

JEFF:  It really is. In California, most households can earn up to about TWICE the federal poverty line and still qualify. In real numbers: about twenty-six hundred dollars a month for a single person, before taxes. About fifty-three hundred a month for a family of four.

SHAWNA:  Fifty-three hundred a month. That's a lot of working families watching right now.

JEFF:  Exactly who it's for. And here's what it's worth: depending on income, a single person can get up to two hundred ninety-eight dollars a month, and a family of four up to nine hundred ninety-four. Most folks land somewhere in between. And even the minimum -- twenty-four dollars a month for one- and two-person households -- buys real groceries every single month.

SHAWNA:  Myth time -- because I've heard this one for years. "I'm on S-S-I, so I can't get CalFresh."

JEFF:  False -- and it's been false since twenty nineteen. S-S-I, that's the monthly federal check for seniors and people with disabilities living on very little. For decades, California really did lock S-S-I recipients out of food benefits -- so a lot of folks "know" the answer is no. That rule is GONE. Since June of twenty nineteen, you can get both. If somebody told you no years ago, it's a new answer now.

SHAWNA:  And seniors in general get a friendlier set of rules, right?

JEFF:  Three ways. If your household has someone sixty or older, the strict up-front income cap doesn't apply -- the county only looks at what's left after key bills, the net number. Second, out-of-pocket medical costs over thirty-five dollars a month actually raise your benefit. And third, a senior who can't shop or cook on their own because of a permanent disability can sometimes count as their OWN household, even while living with family. So don't rule grandma out just because she lives with you -- ask when you apply.

SHAWNA:  Okay -- the honest part. The rules changed this year, didn't they?

JEFF:  They did. Two changes from last year's big federal law. First: since June first, able-bodied adults eighteen through sixty-four need to work, volunteer, or train about twenty hours a week to keep benefits long-term. But hear the second half: the exemption list is long. Parents of kids under fourteen, anyone pregnant, anyone medically unable to work, caregivers, students, and more. Don't assume the work rule hits you -- apply, and the county figures out which rules apply to you.

SHAWNA:  And the second change?

JEFF:  Immigration status. As of April, some immigrants who are here lawfully -- including refugees -- lost FEDERAL CalFresh. California does run a state version, the California Food Assistance Program, with the same benefit amounts -- but it only covers certain groups, like green-card holders in their first five years and survivors of abuse. Right now it does NOT cover most refugees who lost the federal benefit. So if anyone in your household isn't a citizen: don't guess, and don't panic. Using food benefits won't hurt a green-card application -- but get free advice from a legal-aid nonprofit first. And the food bank never asks about status.

SHAWNA:  So how does somebody actually apply?

JEFF:  Four doors -- pick whichever you like. Online, any day of the year, at BenefitsCal dot com. By phone -- the statewide number is on your screen. In person at a county office -- Nevada City or Truckee, both on your screen. Or just dial two-one-one, and a real person walks you through it. Two more things. If you're in a true emergency -- almost no money and no food -- say so, because there's an expedited track that can load benefits within three days. And once you're on, it's not forever-automatic: answer the county's renewal mail so your benefits keep flowing.

SHAWNA:  And before we leave CalFresh -- the card works at the farmers market too?

JEFF:  It does. Your CalFresh card is welcome at the Nevada City farmers market -- Saturdays, downtown on Union Street. And here's the tip: at the market manager's booth, ask whether Market Match is running this season. When it is, they match what you spend -- up to ten extra dollars a visit, free -- for fresh fruits and vegetables. It always pays to ask.

SHAWNA:  One question at the booth could double your produce money. Worth asking.

SHAWNA:  Program two: W-I-C. And I'll confess something -- I always assumed W-I-C was only for moms.

JEFF:  Almost everybody does, and that myth costs kids real food. W-I-C stands for Women, Infants, and Children, and it's free monthly money for healthy groceries -- loaded on its own card, like a debit card. It's for anyone pregnant, anyone who's had a baby in the last six months, breastfeeding parents, and any child under five. And here's the part almost nobody knows: dads, grandparents, foster parents -- if you're raising a child under five, YOU can enroll that child. Grandma raising a grandkid? That kid can get W-I-C.

SHAWNA:  So the child qualifies no matter who's doing the raising. What about income?

JEFF:  Higher than people guess, and it counts everyone in your household. A household of one, about twenty-nine thousand five hundred a year. And careful with that number -- a single parent and one child already count as a household of TWO, and that limit is about forty thousand. A family of four, about sixty-one thousand a year.

SHAWNA:  And you said there's a shortcut.

JEFF:  The best shortcut of the day. If you or your child already have Medi-Cal -- that's California's free or low-cost health coverage -- or CalFresh, or CalWORKs, another state aid program, then you have ALREADY passed the W-I-C income test. Automatically. And bring your Medi-Cal card to the appointment -- it doubles as your proof of income.

SHAWNA:  And what does a family actually get each month?

JEFF:  Two things on that W-I-C card. First, specific healthy staples -- milk, eggs, cereal, beans, baby formula, baby food. Second, flexible produce money for ANY fruits and vegetables you want -- fresh, frozen, or canned: twenty-six dollars a month for each kid ages one to five, forty-eight if you're pregnant, fifty-two if you're breastfeeding. Plus real human support -- nutrition advice and breastfeeding help, included, free. And if you're pregnant, enroll as soon as you know -- the earlier you start, the more months of help you get.

SHAWNA:  Twenty-six dollars of produce a month, per kid -- that adds up fast. Okay: where do folks sign up?

JEFF:  It's run by county Public Health, and it's an appointment, not a waiting line -- phone, video, or in person, your choice. There's an office in Grass Valley and one in Truckee, and both numbers are on your screen. One call, and they'll tell you exactly what to bring.

SHAWNA:  Last stop. Everything so far is an application. What about somebody whose fridge is empty TONIGHT?

JEFF:  Then skip the forms -- this county is genuinely good at this. Two heroes. First: the Interfaith Food Ministry, on Henderson Street in Grass Valley. It works like a drive-through: you pull up, tell the volunteers your picks of the day, and they load your car -- fresh produce, dairy, pantry staples. If you live anywhere in Nevada County and you need food, you qualify. Full stop. There is NO income test. Bring a photo ID and something with your address -- a bill or a lease -- and you can shop once every other week. They're open Monday, Wednesday, and Friday from ten to one -- heads up, the first hour is by reservation, walk-ins start at eleven -- plus Saturdays, no reservation needed. And if you're unhoused -- no address -- you are still welcome. Just say so at check-in.

SHAWNA:  And hero number two?

JEFF:  The Food Bank of Nevada County -- and this one asks for NO paperwork at all. Nothing. No forms, no income questions. They bring the food to you, with mobile distributions all over the county -- second and fourth Thursdays in Grass Valley, every Tuesday in Truckee, plus more sites from North San Juan out to Washington. You show up, you get groceries. And if you physically can't get to a site, they partner with a local group called FREED to deliver to homebound folks at home.

SHAWNA:  And parents -- there's a summer thing happening right now, isn't there?

JEFF:  A great one. All summer, a local group called Community Roots -- the folks who run our school meals -- puts out FREE meals for any kid eighteen and under. No sign-up, no paperwork, no income test. Easiest one: free lunch at the library -- the Grass Valley library and the Madelyn Helling library in Nevada City, weekdays, noon to one, right into August. Just walk in, no card needed -- and there's lunch at the Memorial Park pool, too. Or, if you'd rather stock up, on Thursdays they hand out a whole week of breakfasts and lunches at once -- up at Nevada Union High School, and at Oak Tree Preschool in Nevada City. The full list of sites and times is at communityrootsnc dot org.

SHAWNA:  And there's more for families this summer than the lunch sites -- there's grocery money, too. Actually -- I brought someone who knows about this one firsthand. Everybody, this is my son, Clay.

SHAWNA:  Clay, can you tell everybody about the SUN Bucks card you got in the mail?

[CLAY ANSWERS -- HOLD]

SHAWNA:  The good stuff. Jeff -- what IS this card?

JEFF:  It's called SUN Bucks -- California's summer grocery money for school kids. When school lets out, those free school lunches stop, so the state loads grocery money onto a card to fill the gap: a hundred and twenty dollars for each child, for the summer. You spend it just like a CalFresh card, right at the grocery store.

SHAWNA:  And who gets it?

JEFF:  Here's the best part -- most families don't lift a finger. If your child gets free or reduced-price school lunch, or you're on CalFresh, CalWORKs, or Medi-Cal, the card comes to you automatically. More than three and a half MILLION California kids got signed up that way, and the cards went out back in late May. So check your mail -- like Clay's family, you may already have it in a drawer. And this is money ON TOP of the free summer lunches -- use both.

SHAWNA:  And if a family didn't get a card?

JEFF:  You can still get it -- but there's a deadline. Turn in a school-meal application, or the Universal Benefits Application, to your child's school by August thirty-first. One form at the school office, and a hundred and twenty dollars a child follows. Not sure if your kids are signed up? There's a help line right on your screen.

SHAWNA:  Thanks, bud. One more group before we close: seniors.

JEFF:  Two quick ones for folks sixty and up. If cooking or shopping has gotten hard, Meals on Wheels delivers real meals to your door -- Gold Country Senior Services covers the west side of the county. In Truckee, Sierra Senior Services delivers to seniors who mostly can't get out of the house -- and if that's not quite you, they'll set you up with a group meal instead. Either way, one phone call and they'll figure out what fits. The suggested donation is about four to six dollars a meal -- and it is truly suggested. Nobody is turned away who can't pay. And if you'd rather eat with company: the Sierra Gold Senior Center on Colfax Avenue in Grass Valley serves a hot lunch at noon every Monday, Tuesday, and Thursday. Sixty and up, just show up -- on your first visit they'll have you fill out one quick form.

SHAWNA:  Today we found: CalFresh -- grocery money for households up to about twice the poverty line, and your E-B-T card works at the farmers market too. W-I-C -- produce money and healthy staples for pregnancy and kids under five, dads and grandparents welcome, and automatic if you're on Medi-Cal. And food this very week, with little or no paperwork: Interfaith Food Ministry, the food bank's mobile sites, summer lunches for kids, SUN Bucks grocery money for each school-age child, and Meals on Wheels for seniors.

JEFF:  And the same three doors as always. Dial two-one-one -- here that's two-one-one Connecting Point -- free, confidential, around the clock, English and Spanish, and a real person helps you find food today. Online, go to findhelp dot org and type in your ZIP code. And us: techempower dot org -- click "Join our Discord," our free online chat community, and a real neighbor will help you one-on-one. A CalFresh application, a W-I-C appointment, finding the nearest food distribution -- we answer.
